Facebook Didn't Kill MySpace. MySpace Did

MySpace2011: Sold for a fraction of its priceMissed the shift6 min watch
▶  Watch on YouTubeSubscribe for the next file

The story

In July 2006, for one week, MySpace was the most visited website in America by one research firm's count, ahead of Google and Yahoo. News Corp had bought it a year earlier for $580 million. In 2011, it sold MySpace for about $35 million.

The usual story is that Facebook killed it. But MySpace was already hurting itself: a $900 million ad deal with traffic targets, twice as many ads, a product its own co-founder says lacked focus, and a rival that let others build on its platform. This is the rise and ruin of the social network that taught a generation how to live online.

Three lessons

  1. 1

    Don't sell out your users to pay for growth. When ads and targets come first, the product gets worse, and people notice.

  2. 2

    Focus beats features. MySpace tried to build everything. Facebook did a few things well, and let others build the rest.

  3. 3

    Your users won't wait for you. MySpace's fans left one friend at a time, and by the time it was obvious, it was too late.
